\section{Threshold and corrected sampler}
\label{sec:threshold}

\paragraph*{Flow.}
Per data coordinate, the CLD forward drift is $Bz$ with $z=(x,v)$ and
\begin{equation}
B=\beta\begin{pmatrix}0 & M^{-1}\\ -1 & -\Gamma M^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad \Gamma^2=4M ,
\label{eq:B}
\end{equation}
where the second relation is critical damping~\cite{dockhorn2022cld}.
The defaults of the official code ($\beta=4$, $M^{-1}=4$, $\Gamma=1$, initial velocity variance $\gamma M=0.01$) give $\Sym B$ the eigenvalues $2, -18$.
The flow therefore stretches the direction $u$ (the top eigenvector of $\Sym B$) at the rate $\kappa=2$, and contracts the direction $w$ (the bottom eigenvector, eigenvalue $w_{\min}=-18$).

\paragraph*{Isotropic schedule.}
The forward law is $Z_t=e^{Bt}Z_0+\sigma(t)\varepsilon$ with $\sigma(t)=\smin e^{rt}$, $t\in[0,T]$ and $\sigma(T)=\smax$.
Its covariance satisfies $\dot C=BC+CB^{\top}+G$ with
\begin{equation}
G=2\sigma^2\,(rI-\Sym B).
\label{eq:G}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Ref.~\cite{namba2026threshold}]
\label{prop:threshold}
$G\succeq 0$ if and only if $r\geq\kappa$.
For $r<\kappa$ the marginals cannot be produced by a linear SDE with drift $Bz$ and state-independent noise, and the Anderson reverse SDE with noise $G$ does not exist.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph*{Samplers.}
Let $\Gp$ be $G$ with its negative eigenvalues set to zero, and let $\Gm=\Gp-G\succeq0$.
We compare three reverse samplers, written as forward-time drift and noise and driven by the same score $s=\nabla\log p_t$:
\Sa{} the \emph{clipped} SDE, $Bz-\Gp s$ with noise $\sqrt{\Gp}$;
\Sb{} the \emph{corrected} SDE, $Bz-\tfrac12(G+\Gp)s$ with noise $\sqrt{\Gp}$;
\Sc{} the \emph{probability-flow ODE}, $Bz-\tfrac12 G s$ with no noise.
Sampler~\Sb{} belongs to the standard family of reverse SDEs with arbitrary diffusion $D\succeq0$, drift $Bz-\tfrac12(G+D)s$~\cite{song2021sde}, with $D=\Gp$; sampler \Sc{} is the member with $D=0$.

\begin{proposition}[Ref.~\cite{namba2026threshold}]
\label{prop:exact}
For every $r$, samplers \Sb{} and \Sc{} reproduce the forward marginals.
For $r\geq\kappa$, \Sa{} and \Sb{} are the same equation.
For $r<\kappa$ and Gaussian data, \Sa{} keeps the mean exact and shrinks the covariance.
\end{proposition}

The statement concerns the marginal law at each time and the exact, continuous-time dynamics; individual trajectories are not reproduced, and the time-step error of the numerical integration is removed by step extrapolation (App.~\ref{app:methods}).

Because \Sa{} and \Sb{} coincide for $r\geq\kappa$, \emph{the threshold sitting exactly at $r=\kappa$ is an identity, not a finding}.
What the experiments measure is the size of the damage below $\kappa$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:difference}
For any score field $\shat$, the drift of \Sb{} minus the drift of \Sa{} is $\tfrac12\Gm\shat$, and their noise is the same.
Hence a score error $\delta=\shat-s$ enters the two samplers differently only through $\tfrac12\Gm\delta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\bigl[Bz-\tfrac12(G+\Gp)\shat\bigr]-\bigl[Bz-\Gp\shat\bigr]=\tfrac12(\Gp-G)\shat=\tfrac12\Gm\shat$.
Both noises are $\sqrt{\Gp}$.
\end{proof}

On the CLD block, for $0<r<\kappa$ only one eigenvalue of $G$ is negative, so $\Gm=2(\kappa-r)\sigma^2uu^{\top}$: the noise that \Sa{} discards lies along the stretching direction.
