\section{Methods}
\label{app:methods}

\paragraph*{CLD parameters.}
The values are taken from the configuration files of the official code (\texttt{nv-tlabs/CLD-SGM}, \texttt{configs/default\_cifar10.txt}): $\beta=4$ (constant), $M^{-1}=4$, $\Gamma=2/\sqrt{M^{-1}}=1$ and $\gamma=0.04$, which gives the initial velocity variance $\gamma M = 0.01$~\cite{dockhorn2022cld}.
When $M$ is varied (Sec.~\ref{sec:map}) we keep $\Gamma=2\sqrt M$ and choose $\beta$ so that $\kappa=2$; at fixed $r/\kappa$, $\beta$ does not affect the result.

\paragraph*{Integration.}
The SDEs are integrated with Euler--Maruyama and the ODE with Heun's method~\cite{kloeden1992numerical}, on a uniform grid from $T$ to $0$.
In Secs.~\ref{sec:exact} and \ref{sec:map}, two step sizes ($h\approx0.002$ and $0.001$) share one Brownian path and are combined as $2x(h/2)-x(h)$.
In a study with step sizes from $0.008$ down to $0.0005$, the paired quantities changed by a few per cent between $h=0.002$ and the extrapolated value.
In Sec.~\ref{sec:learned} a single step $h\approx0.002$ is used, and the discretization error cancels in $\Delta$ because the exact-score run shares the path.
The prior is sampled exactly from $p_T$.

\paragraph*{Statistics and pre-registration.}
Error bars are standard errors over seeds; the threshold fit uses a bootstrap over seeds.
For the mixture checks, the learned-score study and the score-weighting study, success criteria and tolerances were written down before any results of the corresponding runs were available. One item, which applied an existing proviso to make the mode-weight check of setting B a reference value only, was added a few seconds after the mixture runs were launched, before any of their results existed. The records, with the file times of the working folder, are in the repository.
Two tolerances of closed-form checks were set after a first look at the values; both are within the pre-registered 5\%.
Single-mode moment equations are solved with an adaptive eighth-order Runge--Kutta method at relative tolerance $10^{-10}$; tightening it to $10^{-12}$ changes $L$ by less than $10^{-6}$ (relative).
